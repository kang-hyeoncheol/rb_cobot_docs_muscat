2.1 Move the robot with jog

On this page

Move the robot with jog ​

Jog moves the robot in small steps only while you hold the button. You use it first when you set teaching points or fine-tune the robot's current position.

In this document you do the following on the Move screen. The three practices are independent of each other, so you can start any one of them in any order.

  • Turn the joints one at a time with Joint jog
  • Move and rotate the TCP with Cartesian (Global) jog
  • Check the speed ratio and the location of the emergency stop

Before you start ​

When operating a real robot

  • Check that there are no people or obstacles around the robot.
  • Find the emergency stop button and operate only when you can press it at any time.
  • Start with a low speed ratio at the bottom of the screen.

Jog modes ​

Choose the jog mode with the tabs at the upper right of the Move screen.

TabWhat movesReference
JointThe angle of one joint (J0 to J6)Each joint axis
GlobalTCP position (X, Y, Z), orientation (RX, RY, RZ) and elbow posture (AA)Robot base frame
LocalTCP position and orientationCurrent tool (TCP) frame
UserTCP position and orientationUser frame

Terms

  • TCP (Tool Center Point): the reference point of the tool attached to the end of the robot.
  • AA (Arm Angle): for a 7-axis robot, the value that says where to place the elbow for the same TCP position and orientation.

How to use ​

Joint jog ​

  1. 1

    Check the connection status

    The green CONNECTED indicator at the top means the UI is connected to the robot. You can operate the robot only while connected.

  2. 2

    Open the Move menu

    Click Move in the left menu to go to the screen where you move the robot.

  3. 3

    Check the current pose

    The upper left shows the robot's current position. X, Y and Z are the TCP (tool center point) position in mm, RX, RY and RZ are its orientation in degrees, AA is the elbow posture (arm angle), and J0 to J6 are the joint angles.

  4. 4

    Choose a jog mode

    Choose the jog mode with the tabs at the upper right. Global, Local and User move the TCP in a straight line or rotate it relative to a coordinate frame, and Joint turns one joint at a time.

  5. 5

    Select the Joint tab

    First, turn the joints directly. Click the Joint tab.

  6. 6

    Turn a joint

    Try the + / − buttons of each joint from J0 to J6. The joint moves only while you hold the button and stops when you release it. Each time you press, the 3D robot rotates and the joint value changes with it. The 3D model and the numbers always show the same robot state. (There may be a very short delay right after you press.)

Cartesian jog ​

  1. 1

    Open the Move menu

    Click Move in the left menu to go to the screen where you move the robot.

  2. 2

    Select the Global tab

    Move the TCP in a straight line. Click the Global tab. Global is the coordinate frame based on the robot base.

  3. 3

    Move along X, Y and Z

    Try the + / − buttons of X, Y, Z, RX, RY, RZ and AA. X, Y and Z move the TCP in a straight line in the direction you press, and the other position values stay almost the same. RX, RY and RZ rotate the TCP about the matching axis of the base frame, so depending on the current pose, angle values other than the one you pressed may change as well. The arm stops instead of going further when the position is out of reach.

Speed ratio and emergency stop ​

  1. 1

    Open the Move menu

    Click Move in the left menu to go to the screen where you move the robot.

  2. 2

    Adjust the speed ratio

    The slider at the bottom is the robot's speed ratio (%), and it also applies to jog speed. Move the slider to change the value. When operating a real robot, start with a low value.

  3. 3

    Remember where the emergency stop is

    The emergency stop button at the upper right is always visible on every screen. When operating a real robot, find it first so you can press it right away in a dangerous situation.
